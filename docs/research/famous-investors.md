# Following famous investors: who actually earned around 20% a year, and can you copy them?

*Research note, 2026-10-06. This is not investment advice. The figures come from web searches of secondary sources and of fund and company documents. Treat every number as approximate. Where sources disagree, the note says so with **[conflict]**.*

## TL;DR

- **Very few people have verifiably compounded at 20% a year or more for decades, and almost none of them still do it in a vehicle you can buy.** The best-known 20%+ records are Lynch, Druckenmiller, Medallion, Greenblatt and early Buffett. They are closed, retired, or were earned on small capital long ago.
- **The investable "compounders" you can buy today return about 14 to 19% a year over the long run, and less over the last 10 years.** Examples: BRK.B, PSH, FFH.TO, MKL. Over the last decade they roughly matched the S&P 500.
- **13F copying works only for a few concentrated, low-turnover managers.** Berkshire is the best documented case, but its evidence is from 1976 to 2006. The products that copy managers in bulk did not beat SPY: GURU lagged, and GVIP's edge came from 2020 to 2021 and then reverted.
- **Politicians' trades:** the old studies (1990s data) found an edge. Later studies (2004 to 2008, and after the STOCK Act) found none. NANC beat SPY by less than 1 percentage point in 2025, mostly because it holds a lot of tech. KRUZ lags.
- **Crypto "wallets" (Arkham and similar) show what famous people hold, live.** They show nothing about skill, and the labels can be wrong.
- **Recommendation for the platform:** track 6 to 8 **shadow portfolios** next to SPY: a Berkshire 13F clone, a TCI clone, a Himalaya clone, a Pershing Square clone, a consensus "best ideas" basket, NANC, GURU, and the listed vehicles (BRK.B, PSH, FFH.TO, MKL). Measure each one from the **13F filing date, not the quarter-end**.

**Takeaways**

1. Records of 20% or more come from early, small-capital periods (early Buffett, Lynch, Greenblatt, early Li Lu) or from vehicles you cannot buy (Medallion, Duquesne).
2. Survivorship bias is large. This list is the winners. Nobody writes "famous investor" profiles of the many concentrated funds that blew up.
3. The realistic net figure for the best investable compounders is **about 15 to 19% long-run and about 12 to 16% over the last decade**. That is roughly the S&P 500, which made about 13 to 15% a year over the same decade.

## 3. Politicians' trades (STOCK Act)

**Reporting lag.** A trade must be reported within 30 days of the member being notified of it, and **no later than 45 days after the trade**. The late fee is only $200, and late filings are common. Disclosures give **dollar ranges, not exact sizes**.

**Academic evidence**

| Study | Data | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Ziobrowski et al. (2004, JFQA) | Senate, 1993–98 | Stocks senators bought beat the market by **85bp/month** |
| Eggers & Hainmueller (2013) | 2004–08 | **No edge.** Members were "rather poor investors" |
| Belmont, Sacerdote et al. (2022, J. Public Econ.) | After the STOCK Act | **No buy-side skill.** Stocks sold by Republican senators underperformed by 50bp over the next 3 months |

**Trackers and ETFs**

- Unusual Whales reports Pelosi-household returns of **+70.9% in 2024** (S&P +24.9%) and **+20.1% in 2025** (S&P +16.6%). These figures are estimated from range disclosures and largely reflect concentrated mega-cap tech and options bets, such as NVDA.
- **NANC** (Democrats' trades) returned **18.54% in 2025 vs SPY's 17.72%**, with a 0.75% expense ratio. Its outperformance comes mainly from being overweight tech.
- **KRUZ** (Republicans' trades) has clearly lagged both NANC and SPY.
- Both launched in Feb 2023, so the record is short.

**Verdict:** the edge seems to be a tech factor tilt, not information. Track NANC as a benchmark; do not trade on individual filings.

## 4. Crypto "wallets"

- On-chain analytics tools label addresses with real-world entities. Examples: Arkham Intelligence, Nansen, Lookonchain.
- Since early 2025, Arkham has labelled the wallets of X accounts with more than 100k followers as "key opinion leaders". Labelled wallets include Justin Sun (one wallet held about $590m), Vitalik Buterin, Arthur Hayes, and Trump-linked wallets (more than $10m in 2024, mostly memecoins and ETH). Funds, ETF custodians and governments are also labelled.
- **What you can see:** balances and transfers in real time. On-chain data has no 45-day lag.
- **What you cannot see or trust:**
  - Labels can be wrong.
  - Activity on exchanges and OTC desks is hidden.
  - Much of it is treasury or custody shuffling, not investment decisions.
  - There is no audited P&L or long-run "history with 20%".
- Following influencer wallets is also a known way to get front-run, or to become exit liquidity for someone else.

**Usefulness:** use it for event alerts, such as large exchange inflows. It is not a track record to copy.

## 5. Does copying work?

| Evidence | Result |
|---|---|
| Martin & Puthenpurackal (2008), copying Berkshire's 13F | Buying in the **month after disclosure** (1976–2006) earned about **10.75–14.3%/yr** in abnormal returns (the figure depends on the method). Berkshire's top 5 holdings averaged 73% of the portfolio. **Caveat:** this is in-sample, from Buffett's best era. BRK itself has been about market-like since about 2005 |
| Verbeek & Wang (2013, JBF), mutual-fund copycats | Copies matched their targets after costs (+0.02%/month). Copying **past winners** did better than most funds |
| Cohen, Polk & Silli, "Best Ideas" | A manager's **highest-conviction** stock beat the market by about **2.8–4.5%/yr**. Their other holdings did not outperform. **Lesson:** copy the top weights, not the whole 13F |
| **GURU** ETF (Jun 2012), copies hedge-fund 13Fs | About **12.2–12.4%/yr vs SPY about 13.7%** over 10 years. **It lagged** |
| **GVIP** (Goldman Hedge Fund VIP, Nov 2016) | Beat SPY mainly in **2020–21**, then gave it back. Close to market otherwise |
| NANC / KRUZ | See section 3: a factor tilt, not alpha |

**Structural problems with copying**

1. **The lag.** Filings arrive up to 45 days after quarter-end. Positions may be up to 4.5 months old by then, which hurts most for high-turnover managers like Duquesne and Appaloosa.
2. **Survivorship and selection bias.** You only hear about managers whose bets worked.
3. **The 13F leaves things out.** Shorts, hedges, cash, non-US stocks and derivatives are missing (beyond listed options). TCI's 13F leaves out its European holdings, and Pabrai's leaves out about 90% of his book.
4. **Scale decay.** Large AUM tends to push returns toward the index (Berkshire, TCI, Pershing).
5. **Regime and person changes.** Buffett has stepped down as CEO and Burry has deregistered.

## 6. Conclusion and what to build

**Candidly:** nobody publicly trackable gives you a credible, investable "20% a year from here". The honest goal is a **shadow-portfolio experiment** that measures, net of the lag, whether any copy beats SPY on this platform's own data.

**Suggested shadow portfolios** (paper only; rebalance on the 13F filing date, around 14 Feb, 15 May, 14 Aug and 14 Nov):

1. **BRK-13F clone.** Top 10 holdings at their 13F weights. This is the best-documented case.
2. **TCI clone.** Concentrated, low turnover, and reported ~18% over the long run.
3. **Himalaya clone.** 8 names, low turnover.
4. **Pershing clone.** Compare it against holding PSH itself, which trades at a ~34% discount. That tests the discount as a source of return.
5. **"Consensus best ideas".** Each manager's #1 to #2 position by weight across the 8 managers, equal-weighted. This applies the Cohen, Polk & Silli result.
6. **Listed compounders.** BRK.B, PSH, FFH.TO and MKL, equal-weighted.
7. **ETF benchmarks.** NANC, KRUZ, GURU and GVIP.
8. **Control.** SPY, plus QQQ, because most of these "edges" are really a tech tilt.

Duquesne and Appaloosa are better used as a **watchlist or idea feed** than as clones, because their turnover is too high. Burry can no longer be followed. Medallion is not investable.
